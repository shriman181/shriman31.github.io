The Problem

Close them, person by person

Paths are dependency-sequenced and time-budgeted to the hours someone genuinely has. Steps they've already outgrown get skipped. If they run ahead or fall behind, the path recalculates and the projected close date moves honestly.

What You Get

Four positions we
won't trade away.

01

Targeting, not content

We will never sell you a course library. There is no shortage of good material and no advantage in adding more to a catalogue nobody browses.

02

Capability, not attendance

Completion rate is the metric that's easy to collect and close to meaningless. We measure whether someone can now do a thing they previously couldn't.

03

The employee owns the profile

Every person sees their own record, can dispute any score, and can opt out of inference entirely. Skills data never touches compensation or discipline.

04

Integrate, never migrate

Every migration we ask for is a reason the project quietly dies in month two. We read from Darwinbox, Keka, Cornerstone and the rest, and write back.

A targeting problem wearing an HR costume.

Mann Agarwal is an Associate Product Manager at Flipkart and an IIT Delhi graduate, Class of '26. At Flipkart he works on systems where the hard part isn't building the feature but deciding who should see it — ranking, targeting, and the unglamorous data plumbing that determines whether a recommendation is useful or noise.

Optimly came out of recognising the same shape of problem inside corporate training. Companies hold every input needed to target learning well — role definitions in the HRMS, competency ratings in performance reviews, real work signal in Jira and GitHub, thousands of licensed course-hours nobody opens. None of it is connected. So training gets assigned by department, because department is the only signal anyone has.

"Every CHRO knows their people need upskilling. Almost none can tell you which employee needs which skill by when. That gap is the entire company."

He is deliberate about what Optimly doesn't do: no content hosting, no LMS replacement, and no causal ROI claim the methodology can't defend. Gap scoring is a visible formula rather than a model output, precisely because the number has to survive a conversation with someone's CFO.

Trust & Data

Skills data is career data.

Personally identifiable data is separated from the skills graph at ingestion, and skill records reference pseudonymous identifiers. Customer data is never used to train models shared across tenants. Benchmarking is opt-in and works on aggregates only — never row-level records.

Our engineering integrations read metadata, not content: commit frequency, repository languages and review participation from GitHub; labels, components and epics from Jira; busy-free density from calendars. Source code is never ingested. Ticket bodies are never stored. Calendar event titles and attendees are never read.
